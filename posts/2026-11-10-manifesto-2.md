# Manifesto 2

#manifesto

I've realised now I've started writing these points that they're really not going
to be in a particularly logical order, but much more "as I remember them" order,
so apologies for that. Anyway, some additional items...

* **Nothing which breaks immersion/cohesion/consistency.** Something about an
instrument as opposed to a studio is how unified it fundamentally is. A violin is
all violin, there's nothing which feels unlike a violin, there's nothing that you
can accidentally grab and suddenly get saxophone vibes. Which leads to...
* **No plugins.** Yes, I realise that's the point at which commercial viability (if
I ever end up caring about that) probably vanishes into the abyss. What?! But you
can't maintain immersion if you're breaking out into completely different styles
of interaction. You can't be consistent when every part of your system might have
different models of how to interact with signals. This is clearly the wrong choice
for most software, but I am still convinced it's right for this.
* **No functionality that isn't deeply considered in terms of where it fits into
the coherent shape of the system.** Everything built-in must be able to show value,
and to show that it can't be achieved in other more consistent ways.

Unpacking my thoughts around those points, I find myself coming back to my original
motivation. The aim is to create a system which rewards understanding and thinking
in the right way - you need to learn it, play it, practice it. It is a learning
experience, as well as an instrument (and an experiment).

I picture the ideal session of "playing" this as something akin to getting lost.
Full screen, control surfaces connected, seamlessly moving between modifying,
tweaking, playing, listening, building a blank space into a performance.

I think that the next set of points might be more technical in nature; I guess
I'll find out when I come to write them!
