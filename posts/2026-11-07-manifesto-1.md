# Manifesto 1

#manifesto

Let's get it out of the way, yes; having a Manifesto is pretentious, I'm aware. But: 
it's as much about me setting out the goals and principles for myself as it is
anything, and that's quite useful. When I started the project I had a lot of
different, and sometimes competing, ideas in mind, and while some of those tensions
have been resolved, it's useful to know which.

In addition, this has to be justifiable to myself at least; building yet another
modular when they already exist is maybe interesting but hard to really justify from
an effort and motivation perspective. So this is a *different* system - it's not going
to optimise for a mass market appeal or try and hit focus-grouped features, it's
fundamentally for me, and if it gets to the point where other people are interested
in using it, then that's something for me to think about then.

Here, then, is the first set of principles which sprung to mind when I started this:

* No analogue recreation. Physical modular synths are constrained by what you can
do electronically, and historically that was largely analogue electronics. The
fundamentals of even modern systems like Eurorack, with CV/Gate, 1V/Oct, and so on,
have evolved over time based on practicality. None of that applies to software, so
why recreate it when you don't need to?
* No physical skeumorphism. Much like the first principle, I'm not interested in
creating a virtual Eurorack system (and frankly there's already an amazing
implementation of that in VCV Rack). I'm not aiming for recreations of real hardware,
I'm not going to try and reinvent dials and switches, surfaces made for manual
manipulation, with no tactility. This even extends to the language - I try and
avoid terms like wires, cables, and so on in favour of more neutral terminology
like connections.
* No comparison with other systems. I'm not going to judge this by how much it
sounds like an ARP2600, or a desk-sized Moog Modular, or any other fetishized
piece of kit. I'm treating it like an instrument. It does what it does, and you can
use it and play it and perform with it within those constraints. It is what it is:
you don't pick up a violin and complain it doesn't sound like a bassoon.

So, those are the first few, which are probably relatively uncontroversial (and if they
are controversial, well - I don't care, it's my synth).

I'll introduce some more over the next few days!
